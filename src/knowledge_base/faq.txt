Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) & General Travel Guides - Al-Nashama Travel Agency
Last Updated: August 2026

1. Airport Transfers and Arrival Services:
- Al-Nashama offers meet-and-assist airport pick-up services. A representative holding a sign with the client's name will meet them inside Queen Alia International Airport before passport control.
- Standard transfer from Airport to Amman hotel: Included in packages with a Private Driver. For standalone transfers, the cost is $45 USD for a standard sedan (up to 3 guests) or $75 USD for a family minivan (up to 6 guests).

2. Private Driver Guidelines:
- Our private drivers are licensed tourist drivers who speak conversational English. They are not historical guides and are not permitted by law to accompany you inside archaeological sites like Petra or Jerash.
- Daily driving hours: The driver is at your disposal for up to 8 hours per day.
- Driver accommodation and meals: When staying overnight outside Amman (e.g. in Petra, Aqaba, or Wadi Rum), the driver's accommodation and meals are covered by Al-Nashama Agency. You do not need to pay for their lodging.

3. Local Tour Guides:
- If you wish to have a guide explain history inside sites, you can hire local guides directly at the visitor centers of Petra (approx. 50 JOD for a standard tour) and Jerash (approx. 20 JOD). For a full-trip accompanying guide, the cost is $150 USD per day.

4. Currency and Tipping Etiquette:
- The local currency is the Jordanian Dinar (JOD). Credit cards are widely accepted in hotels, large restaurants, and malls in Amman and Aqaba, but cash is essential for small shops, local markets, tipping, and in Wadi Rum.
- Tipping is highly appreciated in Jordan as it represents a significant portion of service staff income.
  * Private Driver: Recommended tip is $15 to $20 USD per day for the group.
  * Local Site Guides: Recommended tip is $10 to $15 USD after the tour.
  * Restaurants: 10% is standard if service is not included in the bill.

5. Accessibility and Packing Advice:
- Visiting historical sites like Jerash and Petra involves walking over uneven cobblestones, dirt paths, and steps. Petra involves at least 5-10 km of walking. Wheelchairs can be hired at visitor centers, and golf carts are available inside Petra for elderly guests or those with mobility restrictions.
- Standard dress code: Jordan is a conservative country. Loose-fitting clothing that covers shoulders and knees is recommended, especially when visiting religious sites or walking in downtown Amman.
